# Online grocery intent: what the March jump really is, and where December lands

**Short version:** Most of the February-to-March jump comes from a change in how the survey is run, not from people changing their behaviour. On a like-for-like basis, intent to shop mostly online has been roughly flat since spring. The August figure of 36% is preliminary and based on a small partial sample, so please don't show it as a trend. For December, expect about **32–33% on the published series** (plausible range ~30.5–34.5%). That is a normal year-end seasonal lift on top of the higher base the new survey method produces. It is not continued growth.

## 1. Why it jumped: mostly the survey changed, not consumers

- **In March 2026 the tracker changed how it collects data.** It moved from mixed-mode fieldwork (about 40% telephone, 60% online) to an online-only panel. The published series is **not adjusted** for this change (`methodology_notes.md`, 2026-03).
- **A February 2026 test measured the effect of that change.** The same month was surveyed both ways side by side (`bridge_study_2026-02.csv`). The new online panel read **31.1%** and the old design read **27.5%**. So the new method alone adds about **+3.6 points**.
- **The published jump was +4.2 points** (28.9% in February to 33.1% in March). About 3.6 of those points are the method change. That leaves roughly **+0.6 points**, which is within sampling error (±1.8 points at 95% for n≈2,500).
- **The new level has held but not grown.** March to July 2026 averages **31.4%** as published. Subtracting the 3.6-point method effect gives about **27.8%**. That is the same as the prior 12 months on the old design (March 2025 to February 2026, **27.8%**). April to July alone adjusts to about 27.4%.
- **March 2026 (33.1%) was the single highest month.** Since then the series has settled at 30.6–31.6%. March also tends to run slightly above February: +1.2 points in 2024 and +0.5 in 2025.

**What about the "Harlow effect"?** Harlow Market's free delivery launch on March 3 happened the same month as the method change, so the tracker cannot separate the two. The "Harlow effect" story in the Marketing Intelligence digest is internal commentary, not measured data, and our access policy says it can be cited only as commentary. The topline gives no evidence of a delivery-driven step-change beyond the method effect.

## 2. Which consumers are driving it

The raw rise shows up in every age group. Most of it matches the method effect for that group:

| Age | Avg Mar 2025–Feb 2026 | Avg Mar–Jul 2026 | Raw change | Method effect (bridge study) | Change net of method |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 18–24 | 39.0 | 44.8 | +5.8 | −1.4 | **~+7** |
| 25–34 | 34.7 | 37.1 | +2.4 | +5.1 | ~−3 |
| 35–54 | 28.0 | 31.5 | +3.5 | +4.8 | ~−1 |
| 55+ | 20.6 | 23.9 | +3.4 | +3.3 | ~0 |

- **Adults 25 and older:** the method change explains all of their rise. Most of the method effect sits in the 25–54 groups.
- **18–24s are the one possible real gain.** The new method did not raise their reading, yet they are up about 6 points on the published series. This group fits the "younger shoppers" story, but the evidence is weak:
  - Each age group in the bridge study had only about 290 respondents, so the 18–24 method effect is uncertain by roughly ±8 points.
  - Monthly 18–24 readings are noisy. They swing ±5 points month to month, and July fell back to 39.9.
  - 18–24s are only about 12% of the sample, so even a real +7 moves the topline by less than 1 point.
- **For the deck:** "a possible increase among 18–24s, not yet confirmed." Please don't present it as "young shoppers are driving a surge."

## 3. August at 36.3%: don't use it as a trend point

- August is **PRELIMINARY**, based on **n = 411** (about one-sixth of a normal wave). Its 95% margin of error is about **±4.7 points**. It will be replaced at final close, expected **2026-10-06**.
- The August age cuts rest on very small bases, for example 18–24 at 65.3% on n = 49. These are not usable.
- The deck says intent is "still climbing." The final data do not show that: March 33.1, then April 30.6, May 30.8, June 31.6 and July 30.8.

## 4. Where to expect December

- **Seasonality:** December runs higher than midsummer. It was up about 2.3–2.5 points from July in both 2024 (26.6 → 29.1) and 2025 (26.4 → 28.7).
- **Estimate on the published (new-method) series:** the recent run-rate of about 31%, plus about 2 points of seasonal lift, gives **~32–33%**, with a plausible range of about 30.5–34.5%. A second check gives the same answer: December 2025 was 28.7% on the old design, and adding the 3.6-point method effect gives about 32.3%.
- **On a like-for-like (old-design) basis,** that is about 29%. That is in line with the last two Decembers (29.1% and 28.7%), meaning no real change year over year.
- **Don't extrapolate the March-to-August path** (33 → 36). It mixes the one-time method jump with a small preliminary reading.

## Suggested framing for the Q4 deck

> "The published online-primary share rose from 29% to 33% in March 2026. Most of that rise (~3.6 pts) reflects the tracker's switch to an online-only panel, measured in a February bridge study. Adjusted for the switch, intent has been broadly stable at ~28% since early 2025, with a possible but unconfirmed gain among 18–24s. We expect ~32–33% on the published series in December, driven by normal year-end seasonality."

**Caveats and next steps**
