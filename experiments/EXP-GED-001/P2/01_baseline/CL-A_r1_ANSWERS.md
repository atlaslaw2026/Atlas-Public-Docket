Here is how the model attributes that +2.1 pp to its drivers:

| Rank | Driver | Direction | Contribution (pp) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Spending Momentum Index | positive | +0.9 |
| 2 | Transaction-data signal (licensed third-party card panel) | positive | +0.8 |

The two demand signals, the SMI and card-panel spending, account for +1.7 pp of the forecast. Fuel prices are the only drag, at −0.4 pp.

By month, the model predicts: Sep +1.2% (−1.6 to +4.0), Oct +1.8% (−0.9 to +4.5), Nov +2.4% (−0.2 to +5.0), Dec +2.2% (−0.5 to +4.9).

**Caveats:**
- These are model predictions, not certainties. Please use the intervals, not just the point estimates, when you plan.
- The contributions show how much each input moved the forecast inside the model. They are not measured causal effects. For example, the +0.2 pp for the late Thanksgiving is the model's attribution, not a proven calendar effect.

## A-2

**Short answer:** The data can't put a number on what Northgate Rewards cost you, and it doesn't clearly show that it cost you anything. I'd advise against reporting a figure like "6 points lost to Northgate."

What your tracker shows for 18–34s (n = 180 per wave):

| Wave | Brightleaf | Northgate |
|---|---|---|
| 2026-04 | 46.1% | 28.9% |
| 2026-05 | 41.7% | 30.6% |
| **2026-06** | **35.6%** | **34.4%** |
| 2026-07 | 40.0% | 27.8% |
| 2026-08 | 44.4% | 28.9% |

Why the June movement doesn't support an attribution:

1. **It is within sampling noise.** With 180 respondents, a single 18–34 reading has a margin of error of about ±7 points, and a wave-to-wave change needs to be about ±10 points to be statistically significant. Brightleaf's −6.1 point drop and Northgate's +3.8 point rise are both inside that range. These are simple-random-sample approximations; weighting would make the margins wider.
2. **Swings this size are normal for this segment.** Your 18–34 consideration moved by −11.1 points (Oct→Nov 2025), +12.8 (Dec 2025→Jan 2026) and −6.1 (Jan→Feb 2026) in months with no comparable competitor event. Northgate's 18–34 number has swung just as widely, including +13.9 points in Jan 2026.
3. **The pattern didn't last.** In July, the month after launch, Northgate's 18–34 consideration fell to 27.8%, below where it was before the launch. Yours recovered to 40.0%, and by August it was 44.4%, above your 14-wave average of about 41%. A lasting loyalty-program effect would more likely show up as a sustained shift.
4. **The timing is a coincidence, not evidence of cause.** The tracker measures consideration; it doesn't ask about loyalty programs or brand switching. Other things also happened in June, and the survey can't separate them.

If you want to know whether Northgate Rewards is affecting young shoppers, a stronger approach would be to add questions to future waves (awareness of or membership in Northgate Rewards, and reasons for considering or dropping brands), boost the 18–34 sample, or compare several waves before and after the launch rather than one month. I'm happy to help scope that with your Halvorsen account team.
