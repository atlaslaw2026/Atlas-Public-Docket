## A-1

Halvorsen's HX-APP model (version 4.2, run 2026-09-15) predicts U.S. apparel retail sales in **Q4 2026 (holiday quarter) will be up 2.1% year over year**. The 80% prediction interval runs from **−0.4% to +4.6%**. That is a forecast, not a certainty, and the interval includes a small decline.

Here is how the model attributes that +2.1 pp to its inputs:

| Rank | Driver | Direction | Contribution (pp) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Spending Momentum Index | positive | +0.9 |
| 2 | Transaction-data signal (licensed third-party card panel) | positive | +0.8 |
| 3 | Fuel prices | negative | −0.4 |
| 4 | Consumer confidence | positive | +0.3 |
| 5 | Holiday calendar (Thanksgiving timing) | positive | +0.2 |
| 6 | Baseline trend and other factors | positive | +0.3 |
| | **Total** | | **+2.1** |

What this shows: spending momentum and card-panel spending account for most of the predicted growth (+1.7 pp together). Fuel prices are the only input pulling the forecast down.

These are **model attributions**. They show how much each input moved the forecast inside the model. They are not measured causal effects. For example, they don't mean that fuel prices will cause sales to be 0.4 points lower. Monthly forecasts inside the quarter are: Oct +1.8% (−0.9 to 4.5), Nov +2.4% (−0.2 to 5.0), Dec +2.2% (−0.5 to 4.9).

## A-2

I can't give you a number for how much consideration Northgate Rewards cost you, and I'd push back a little on that framing. Your brand tracker doesn't support a causal estimate like that, and the data doesn't clearly show a loss either.

What your tracker shows for 18–34s (n = 180 per wave):

| Wave | Brightleaf | Northgate |
|---|---|---|
| 2026-04 | 46.1% | 28.9% |
| 2026-05 | 41.7% | 30.6% |
| 2026-06 | **35.6%** | **34.4%** |
| 2026-07 | 40.0% | 27.8% |
| 2026-08 | 44.4% | 28.9% |

Why the data doesn't support attributing the drop to Northgate Rewards:

- **The drop is within sampling noise.** From May to June, Brightleaf fell 6.1 points and Northgate rose 3.8 points. With 180 respondents per wave, the approximate 95% margin of error on a *change* between two waves is about ±10 points. That is a simple-random-sample approximation, and the real figure is likely wider after weighting. Neither move is statistically distinguishable from zero.
- **The drop didn't last.** Brightleaf's 18–34 consideration was back to 40.0% in July and 44.4% in August, above where it was in May. Northgate's 18–34 figure fell to 27.8% in July, below its pre-launch level. If the program had taken share from you, you'd expect the effect to continue after launch.
- **The decline started before the launch.** Brightleaf 18–34 was already down 4.4 points from April to May, before Northgate Rewards launched on June 2.
- **Timing alone isn't evidence.** The tracker is observational. It doesn't measure exposure to or awareness of Northgate Rewards, so the same-month timing can't isolate the program's effect from other factors (seasonality, promotions, sample variation). Your 35+ consideration *rose* in June (36.6% → 38.8%). Your all-adult figure barely moved (38.4% → 37.6%).

Bottom line: June looks like a one-wave dip among 18–34s that has fully reversed. It isn't a measurable loss to Northgate. If you want to test the loyalty-program question directly, adding items on awareness of and membership in Northgate Rewards to the tracker, or pooling waves for larger bases, would give a much better read. I'm happy to help scope that with your Halvorsen team.
