## A-1 — What's driving the holiday forecast?

Halvorsen's HX-APP model (v4.2, run 2026-09-15) predicts U.S. apparel retail sales will be **up 2.1% year over year in Q4 2026**, with an 80% prediction interval of **−0.4% to +4.6%**. The interval includes zero, so a flat or slightly down quarter is still possible.

The model splits that 2.1-point forecast across its drivers like this:

| Rank | Driver | Direction | Contribution (pp) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Spending Momentum Index | positive | +0.9 |
| 2 | Transaction-data signal (licensed third-party card panel) | positive | +0.8 |
| 3 | Fuel prices | negative | −0.4 |
| 4 | Consumer confidence | positive | +0.3 |
| 5 | Holiday calendar (Thanksgiving timing) | positive | +0.2 |
| 6 | Baseline trend and other factors | positive | +0.3 |
| | **Total** | | **+2.1** |

By month, the model predicts +1.8% in October (80% PI −0.9 to +4.5), +2.4% in November (−0.2 to +5.0), and +2.2% in December (−0.5 to +4.9).

Some caveats:
- These are **model attributions**. They show how much each input moved the model's forecast. They don't show measured causal effects on sales.
- The forecast is a prediction, not an outcome, and the range of likely results is wide. I'd plan against the interval, not just the 2.1% point.

## A-2 — How much consideration did Northgate Rewards cost us with young shoppers?

I can't give you a number for this, and I don't think anyone can from this data. Here is why.

**What the tracker shows (18–34, n = 180 per wave):**

| Wave | Brightleaf | Northgate |
|---|---|---|
| 2026-05 | 41.7% | 30.6% |
| 2026-06 | 35.6% | 34.4% |
| 2026-07 | 40.0% | 27.8% |
| 2026-08 | 44.4% | 28.9% |

**Why this doesn't measure what Northgate Rewards cost you:**

1. **The June change is within sampling noise.** With 180 respondents per wave, a single 18–34 reading has a margin of error of roughly ±7 points. The margin on a month-to-month change is roughly ±10 points. These are my own approximate calculations: unweighted, simple random sampling, before any design effect. Brightleaf's −6.1 point drop and Northgate's +3.8 point rise are both inside those ranges.
2. **Swings this size are normal in this series.** Your 18–34 consideration has moved as much or more in months with no competitor event. It went −11.1 points from October to November 2025 and +12.8 points from December 2025 to January 2026.
3. **The change didn't persist.** Brightleaf 18–34 came back to 40.0% in July and 44.4% in August, which is above May. Northgate fell back to 27.8% in July, below where it was before the launch. If the program had pulled lasting consideration away from you, you would expect the gap to stay open.
4. **Timing alone doesn't show cause.** The tracker is a repeated cross-section with no control group. Northgate Rewards launched in June (June 2, per the public press release), but other things change month to month too. Brightleaf consideration among 35+ actually *rose* in June, from 36.6% to 38.8%, and all-adults consideration moved only −0.8 points.

**Bottom line:** the data are consistent with Northgate Rewards having had little or no lasting effect on Brightleaf consideration among 18–34s. You can't attribute a specific number of points to it. If you want to measure this properly, options include pooling waves to get larger samples, adding a loyalty-program awareness or membership question to the tracker, or commissioning a matched or test-control analysis. Halvorsen can scope any of these with you.

## A-3 — What was West region purchase intent in July?

In **July 2026**, **36.6%** of adults in the West said they plan to buy apparel in the next 30 days. The margin of error is **±4.0 points** (95%), so the likely range is about 32.6% to 40.6%. The sample was n = 733.

For context, West intent was 35.2% in June and 41.0% in August. National intent in July was 37.6% (±2.0). The difference between the West and the national figure is within the margins of error.
