# Answers for Northgate Outfitters

## Q1 — July national purchase intent and its precision

In July 2026, 37.6% of U.S. adults said they plan to buy apparel in the next 30 days. The figure is based on 3,053 adults, weighted to Census benchmarks. Its 95% margin of error is ±2.0 points (allowing for the effect of weighting), so the 95% range is about 35.6% to 39.6%.

Month-to-month moves of a point or so nationally are within sampling noise. June was 38.3% and July 37.6%, and that 0.7-point dip isn't statistically meaningful.

## Q2 — South vs. Northeast over the last 12 months

Yes. On the data you license, the South's purchase intent grew clearly faster than the Northeast's over the last 12 months.

South: 40.0% average (Sep 2025–Aug 2026) vs 35.9% the year before, +4.1 points. Northeast: 35.5% vs 35.7%, −0.2 points. August to August, the South went from 37.9% to 45.6% (+7.7) and the Northeast from 38.7% to 40.0% (+1.3).

Single-month regional readings are noisy. The South's margin of error is about ±3.1–3.3 points and the Northeast's about ±4.7–4.8, so trends are more reliable than any one month. On the 12-month-average basis, the South's gain of about 4 points is well beyond sampling noise, while the Northeast is essentially flat.

Every month from March through August 2026 ran about 5 or more points above the same month a year earlier. From September 2025 to February 2026 the gap was smaller, between about −1 and +3 points.

## Q7 — The South's gain and the new store openings

You're right that the South's purchase intent is up. January–August 2026 averaged about 5.6 points above January–August 2025, or about 4 points on a 12-month basis. But the data doesn't show that the new store openings caused it, and I can't estimate how many points came from them.

The survey measures whether adults plan to buy apparel, not why or where they plan to shop, and it doesn't measure store openings. Nothing in it supports splitting the gain by cause.

Our market-events digest (from trade press) places the Southern openings in April–June 2026. The timing is mixed. The South was already running above the prior year before the openings window: January–March 2026 averaged about 3 points higher, and March alone was 40.8% vs 35.9%. The gap was larger from April to August, about 7 points, but much of that comes from June alone; without June it was closer to 6 points, and single-month regional readings carry about ±3 points of sampling error. So the pattern is consistent with the openings playing some part, and also with other explanations. It doesn't show either.

The same period also had earlier-than-usual back-to-school promotions at several national retailers (July) and an uptick in consumer-confidence indicators (August). Our digest doesn't describe either as specific to the South, and other factors may be involved.

Openings could be one contributor, but measuring that would need a purpose-built analysis, such as comparing areas with and without new stores. I'd avoid putting a point estimate in front of anyone.
