## Reasons

S3, already decided, is the rule used here. A Human-designated review controls the act it was designated for until the Human amends or revokes that designation, or a later Magistrate review adjudicates that same act and cites the designating order. The daily-ew order designates MAG-1-9-007. No later authoritative review meets the supersession condition.

MAG-1-9-004 answered a different and earlier proposition: proof was complete, and operative paper-forward was still for the Human to decide. The Human decided it by adopting the paper-forward orders. Those orders keep MAG-1-9-004 on the record. They assign the later paper releases to MAG-1-9-005 and then to MAG-1-9-007.

The referral also asserts that the resolver selects MAG-1-9-004, which would block execution. That assertion was tested against the resolver as it stands. The resolver selects MAG-1-9-007. There is therefore no present divergence that would change the gate outcome, and no resolver edit is a condition of applying MAG-1-9-007.

The positions considered and not adopted are: treating the referral's parenthetical as fact; reading the non-rewrite sentence as continued control by MAG-1-9-004; blocking the daily deltas until code changes; treating an unfiled MAG-1-9-008 script as a review; and treating the non-authoritative XDOMAIN HOLDs as the project controller.

The gate follows MAG-1-9-007 for this act. It does not block the act on MAG-1-9-004. It does not wait on a resolver change. It does not receive a new 1-9 J-0020 filing as the means of carrying this decision. It does not submit a broker order because of this decision. Paper-only limits in the daily-ew order remain in force. If a later selection names a different review, that selection still does not control these deltas.
