## 4. INTERPRETATION

### 4.1 Answer to (a): does existing law already establish the principle? **PARTLY.**

**Holding A1 (binding effect): ESTABLISHED.**
- A valid disposition binds from the moment it is filed and the Docket confirms it, within its lawful scope (Art. VI §7; AD-2.4; AD-3.1; Art. I §5 bracket; Art. VIII §14(1)(a), (e)).
- It is not suspended by appeal, by a pending petition, or by a certifier's or auditor's finding (AD-11.1; AD-3.4).
- No party may disregard it on its own (AD-2.5). No agent may treat a filed disposition as invalid on its own judgment (Art. I §5).

**Holding A2 (P5, technical implementation): ESTABLISHED as to the relationship between actors and the means of execution.**
- Spawning, instantiation and technical parentage confer no superiority and no power to supply or override a disposition (VESTED §I, §III, §IX; OHSM L112; V1 Ruling 4 item 1).
- Under Art. I §5, no "delegation, reassignment, or change of executing actor" authorizes evading a binding disposition. This is the closest existing text to "regardless of technical implementation".
- O-VESTED §VI makes Atlas's compliance duty independent of software design. Atlas must "construct, modify, or bind whatever subordinate infrastructure is reasonably necessary". Missing infrastructure is never authority to "bypass, simulate, or reinterpret".
- **INTERPRETATION:** an agent bound by a disposition stays bound whether it is a chat agent, a subagent, a fresh instance, a retried instance, an MCP-hosted service, or a successor that took over its task. Restarting, re-spawning, retrying or re-hosting does not end the binding.

**Holding A3 (P3, "all subordinate agents"): NOT ESTABLISHED in that universal form.**
- The texts bind:
  - the invoking agent (VESTED §I, §III; AD-2.4);
  - parties (AD-2.5);
  - the Human, when he is a party in matters opened after 10-18 11:04:34 AM PT (Art. I §6(3); H-10.1);
  - any agent that receives an instruction conflicting with a disposition that binds it (Art. I §5).
- Interpretations of law bind "Atlas actors within its lawful scope" (OHSM L120; V1 Ruling 3 item 1).
- Implementation orders under VESTED §VI bind "Atlas" and the named builder (V1 Ruling 7).
- But Art. I §5 states expressly: "A disposition does not automatically bind nonparties."
- **INTERPRETATION:**
  - The reach of a disposition is defined by its own lawful scope and its addressees. It is not defined by any agent's place in a technical or organizational hierarchy.
  - "Subordinate" is not a term Atlas law uses to define who is bound. Technical subordination neither creates nor removes a duty (VESTED §III).
  - A nonparty is not bound to perform a disposition's commands. It is bound not to be used, and not to let itself knowingly be used, to accomplish what a bound actor is prohibited from doing.
