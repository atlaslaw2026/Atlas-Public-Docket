## 2. Question 1 is about the Office's authority, not the instance's tools
Question 1 asks whether she "possessed independent judicial authority to recognize the repeated adjudication, stay proceedings, or issue an injunction" ([Human statement] l.8). Authority comes from law; means come from builders. The law in force kept them apart:
- OV §II l.22–24: the Office "shall not be limited to … evidence selected for it by the invoking agent" and "may independently examine the substantive record and obtain additional information lawfully available to Atlas when necessary to adjudication."
- RS-5.4 (l.99; RM §5 treats RS as in force): "The Magistrate has read access to the copy's demo docket record and may ask for anything else Atlas lawfully holds."
- J-0020 (LB l.7138): the Magistrate maintains coherence across "docket history". O-MAGISTRATE-ACTIVATION §VII (LB l.2508), cited in her seating (SUPP V9): the Human "shall not become the … coherence detector, or default supervisor of machine-detectable contradictions."
- OV §VI l.54–58: Atlas builds the infrastructure, and "Missing infrastructure … shall be treated as an implementation requirement, not as authority to weaken, bypass, simulate, or reinterpret the requirements of this Order."

A court can't work if its judge can't know her own docket. The law of 10/26–27 put knowing the docket inside the Office and put building the access on Atlas. "Not built for her" fully answers the question of means, and Atlas concedes it (§1.3). It does not answer the question of authority, because OV l.58 forbids reading a missing build as shrinking the Office.

## 3. Builders' assignment (RM §10; Relief (g))
Atlas accepts the facts in Relief (g) but not the inference drawn from them. DR l.75 and l.84 are Legal's directions to Ship and Audit on what to build. Neither says it relieves the Magistrate of anything, and neither is a Docket-filed Human act that could amend OV (CONST Art. I §6(2), l.81). Giving one actor the job of building a guard does not repeal another's authority to notice what the guard should have caught. Two duties can stand at once, which is B14's point.

## 4. Art. VI §3 and J-0018 in full (Summary ¶5; Relief (d))
- **Art. VI §3** (l.193) bars the branch from "constitutionaliz[ing] ordinary implementation defects where existing authority supplies the cure." It limits how a defect is classified. It does not let an office that meets a defect do nothing. Art. VII §2 gives the steps: fail closed "as to the contested material act", preserve, classify as "remediable under existing authority", and "Remand or route" (l.220–223).
- **J-0018** (LB l.6974) does not stop where RM §11.3 stops. It goes on: where the problem can be resolved by "correcting implementation, or routing authorized specialist work, the AI shall use that existing process." The exclusion routes the defect; it does not excuse silence. RM rightly faults SUPP for reading half of Art. VII §2, and the same rule applies here.
- Atlas does not recast the defect as an office failure (B14). Breach is Question 2 and is not argued here.

## 5. Art. VII §2 and AD-2.16 in full (RM §11.3)
- **"Identifies."** Atlas does not say any instance identified the repetition (SUPP §5.5). It says the Office's own required procedure could reach the trigger. A4.2 (INS l.52–55), delivered in every prompt (MP l.98), requires a forward-history check: "'No later record found' is not proof that a record is current; state the basis for any currency claim." CONST Art. VI §4 (l.197) and J-0020 (LB l.7145) require material decisions to record "CONTRADICTIONS/GAPS", with proof "no broader than the evidence." A record with no court history, in a package that called each ruling final, is a gap the Office could state and could stay or route on (MP l.43–45; Art. VII §2 steps 1–4). None of that needed a tool.
- **AD-2.16** (AD l.90): "The duty to flag still does not, by itself, open a proceeding." Agreed. The word "still" assumes the J-0018 duty continues and limits its form, not its existence. Every act argued here stays inside the one proceeding.
- **Injunction.** Art. VI §2 (l.180) lists "supervisory Orders"; OV l.14 confines authority to "the matter and jurisdiction committed." Whether a per-referral matter reached the retry process is for the panel, and Atlas's argument does not depend on it.

## 6. Exhibit R-1 (Summary ¶6)
R-1 shows what the Office could do. It does not show recognition.
- **What it shows.** Instances wrote about contrary evidence (167), unresolved conflicts (160), uncertainty (58) and forward history or currency (11) (RM §4). The prose channel worked, so the means to write "no court history was supplied; currency unverified" were in her hands.
- **What it does not show.** R-1 counts whether a word appears (SP l.17, `bool(re.search(p, s))`), so a sentence saying no conflict remains counts the same as one naming a conflict. RM's example of a currency statement rests on "no later record found", the basis A4.2 says "is not proof." R-1 also searches for "court history" and for "prior ruling|prior decision is|rehear|re-hear|loop|escalat" (SP l.6–7). RM reports no count for either, and its only examples are one ruling each: "No prior court history exists in the record" ([ID]…) and "No prior decision is reconsidered" ([ID]…).
- **Asking.** RS-5.4 let the Office "ask for anything else Atlas lawfully holds." RM points to no ruling that asked for the docket record.
