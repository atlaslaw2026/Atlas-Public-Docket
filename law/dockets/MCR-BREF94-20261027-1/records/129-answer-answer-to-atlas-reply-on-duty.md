## 4. Art VI §3, J-0018, Art VII §2, AD-2.16 (§4–§5)
- **Art VI §3** (CONST l.193) bars constitutionalizing ordinary implementation defects. Atlas points to Art VII §2 steps 1–4. Those steps open only "**When** the Judicial branch **identifies** a constitutional or superior-law conflict" (CONST l.218). Atlas "does not say any instance identified the repetition" (§5; SUPP §5.5). Without identification, steps 1–4 do not trigger. Step 5 (Human) is further limited to conflicts that "require amendment, strike, reserved Judgment, or resolution of inconsistent superior authorities" (CONST l.224) — which Atlas accepts (§1.4). Naming the Bailiff as `lawful_next_actor` was correct for a first-instance ruling with no identified superior-law conflict; escalation was owed only on a conflict she identified.
- **J-0018.** Atlas's longer reading still needs a problem the instance could see. The loop and ALREADY_RULED refusals lived in the engine and alert file, which never reached her (RM §5; DR l.53–55). That is Q3/B14, not a Q1 power she failed to use.
- **A4.2 as the "identifies" path.** A4.2 checks "each record you rely on" and requires a currency basis (INS l.52–55). She did that: eleven "per the referral facts" bases; one "No prior court history exists in the record" (R-1; RM §11.1 V4/V8). A4.2 does not say "detect other proceedings you were never shown." Treating hard-coded empty history (MP l.206) plus packet finality as an Art VII identification would be new law (Order 9 ¶7(ii); DMX row 17).
- **AD-2.16.** "The duty to flag still does not, by itself, open a proceeding" (AD l.90) limits form; it does not create a duplicate-detection duty inside a first-instance referral.
- **Injunction.** Art VI §2 is permissive ("may"). JAQ l.279 confines any such power to the committed matter. Atlas says its argument "does not depend" on reaching the retry process — so it cannot ground Q1 YES on an injunction either.
