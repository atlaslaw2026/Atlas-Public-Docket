## 3. Objection
(1) **The pattern exceeded sound discretion.** Each new round was small. Taken together, though, they turned a fixed schedule (O3 L6) into a moving target: six new rounds in 89 minutes, one admitted reopening (O6-A L4), and a disposition that was promised to "wait" for every new window (O3-A L5, O7 L18). A party that is told the record is closing should be able to rely on that. The Human has said speed matters (O8 L3: "Clinton's point is well taken"). Open-ended rounds defeat both finality and speed, even when each one is well meant. INFERRED (argument).

(2) **Atlas has no written close-of-record rule.** A text search of the Law Book (e820caac…) found no rule that says when a Magistrate record closes or what it takes to reopen one: INFERRED (the search found nothing; that does not prove no rule exists). The nearest text is a non-Magistrate rule on remands (L2669, "Block repeated identical remands from looping"). Atlas's one stated standard period is 72 hours (Order 1 L22). Because no rule exists, every close is only as firm as the next order makes it. That is a gap in the law, not a fault of one instance.

## 4. Relief requested
(a) **No delay.** Leave Part 1 and the P4 ruling exactly on their current timing.
(b) **A finding for this matter.** In this matter, no further round of filing (supplement, note, answer or reply) may open unless a written motion shows new record facts that could not have been filed earlier, and good cause.
(c) **A standing rule, labeled PROPOSED PRACTICE.** It is not Atlas law until Clinton adopts it first-hand:
> **Close of record (proposed).** 1. An order that closes the record says so in those words and names a fixed disposition time. That time does not move except under item 4. 2. After the close, no party may file a supplement, note, answer or reply unless a written motion first shows new facts that could not have been filed before the close. 3. Each side gets at most one answer to any filing allowed under item 2, and there is no answer to an answer. 4. The Magistrate may reopen the record, on motion or on her own, only by a written order that gives the reason and the new facts, limits the reopening to those facts, and sets a new fixed disposition time. 5. Waivers of a window take effect when filed.
