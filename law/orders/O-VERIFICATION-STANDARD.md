## Ruling

Probability gives answers. Verification gives truth.

No claim relied upon under Atlas shall rest on a middleman where the witness is reachable. The witness is the party that has to be right about the claim: the directory for membership, the filing for the company's statements, the business itself for its own facts. AI-generated answers — including AI Overviews, chat assistants, and search summaries — are LEAD material under O-LIBRARIAN-SOURCE-ADMISSIBILITY, never TRUSTED, no matter how confident the presentation or how real the citations look. A citation that does not support the claim is decoration.

Where the witness cannot be reached directly, Hunter shall say so, label the claim accordingly, and never fill the gap with generation.

Speed is not verification. A system that must answer in a second structurally cannot perform the slow check; its output shall be treated with the discount its economics require.

IT IS SO ORDERED.

## Demonstration

2026-09-23: Human's cold question — is BOA Steakhouse Santa Monica on the InKind app? — with a conflicting Google AI "yes" revealed only after the check. Hunter-method verification against InKind's own directory: exactly one BOA Steakhouse partner (Las Vegas); Santa Monica absent. Google's AI returned both "yes" and "no" for near-identical phrasings; a second Google AI returned "no" for the same query; the original "yes" flipped to "no" on re-query sixteen minutes later. The business, asked directly by the Human, said no. Human verdict: the system succeeded in this instance — right answer, plus why the wrong answer looked tempting. Recorded as PUB-M-008.

## Implementation

Standing rule for all agents operating under Atlas: identify the witness before answering; check the witness; label what could not be checked. Phrasing-variance and time-variance instability in AI answers is expected and shall be documented, not averaged.
